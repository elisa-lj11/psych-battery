What is Mental Meter?

A real-time dashboard that estimates your cognitive energy throughout the day — not from how you feel, but from what your computer has actually seen you do.

The core idea
Your brain has a fuel tank. Meetings, deep focus, context switching, and late hours drain it. Sleep, walks, food, and downtime refill it. Mental Meter tracks that tank in real time using a two-variable dynamical system (energy + stress) driven by ActivityWatch data.
Where the data comes from
ActivityWatch runs locally on your machine and watches which apps and windows are active. Mental Meter reads it every 5 minutes to estimate meeting load, typing intensity, focus duration, and context switching — then feeds those into the energy model.
The circadian layer
On top of your activity, your body has a natural 24-hour rhythm. The model shifts your resting energy baseline up or down depending on the time of day and your chronotype (lark, intermediate, or owl).
Three display layers
Tap the meter to go deeper. Layer 0 is just the fill. Layer 1 opens the diagnostics view: circadian curve, state tanks, energy dynamics, and phase portrait. Layer 2 adds the signal breakdown: pressure, drain rows, recovery rows, and the legacy activity window.
Research context
This tool is part of ongoing research at UC Berkeley on passive sensing and cognitive self-monitoring. The model is experimental — treat the number as a useful signal, not a medical measurement.

Glossary

E (Energy)
Your mental fuel tank on a 0-100 scale. When it drops, the meter shrinks; sleep, walks, food, and people refill it.
S (Stress)
A second tank that rises under cognitive load like stacked meetings, late-night work, or heavy multitasking. High stress makes energy drain faster.
Pressure
The load on you right now: meeting density, message volume, and app switching. When pressure stays high, stress climbs.
Chronotype
Your natural body-clock type: lark, intermediate, or owl. It sets when your daily energy baseline tends to peak.
MSF_sc
Sleep-corrected mid-sleep on free days, derived from the Munich Chronotype Questionnaire. We use it to place your chronotype curve.
Peak cognition
The part of the day when your circadian baseline is highest. Use it for demanding work when possible.
Recovery
Anything that adds energy back: outside time, exercise, social contact, food, or rest. Use recovery to push the meter upward.
Drain
Anything that pulls energy out: meetings, deep focus, context switching, late hours, or high typing load. High drain makes the meter fall faster.
ActivityWatch
A local time tracker that watches your active windows on your machine. We read it to estimate how much you meet, focus, and switch.
Model B
The two-tank math that updates energy and stress every tick. It turns recovery, drain, pressure, and resting baseline into meter movement.
Model D
The circadian resting baseline E_rest over 24 hours. It says what "rested" should feel like at this time of day.
Demo mode
A frontend-only simulation with no backend writes. Use it to try activities and watch the model respond in real time.

How it works

  1. Your energy level rises and falls through the day: drains pull it down, while rest, walks, food, and people refill the meter.
  2. A second tank tracks stress — back-to-back meetings and long focus sessions fill it, and a full stress tank makes energy drain faster.
  3. On top of all that, your body has a natural daily rhythm — your circadian baseline — that quietly lifts or lowers what "rested" means at this hour.
  4. Every five minutes the app reads what you actually did (meetings, focus blocks, walks, sleep) and updates both tanks a little.
  5. The meter fill shows your current energy level, smoothed; the detail layers show the math underneath if you want to look.

How the Mental Meter model works

Your mental energy is modeled as two coupled tanks driven by what you actually do across the day. Six knobs explain almost everything you'll see.

Energy (E)

The main meter fill shows how much capacity you have left. Recovery activities lift it; drains pull it down. It also drifts back toward your resting baseline over time.

dE/dt = recovery − drain − k·(E − E_rest)

Stress (S)

A second tank that builds under cognitive pressure (deep work, meetings, late-night focus). High S accelerates drain on E and shortens its recovery half-life.

dS/dt = pressure_in − decay·S

Pressure

The instantaneous load on you right now — meetings stacked, message volume, switching between tools. It's the input to S, not a tank itself. Think of it as "how loud the day is."

How activities change things

Each activity card flips a small set of feature inputs (right-side panel). Those flow through the same Model B + D math the live app uses. The right panel only shows the features the activity actually moves — others stay neutral.

The timeline

The strip across the bottom starts with live E and S meters, then plots E (green) and S (red) across the simulated 24h day. Each icon marks an activity you picked.
