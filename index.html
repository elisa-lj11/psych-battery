How the model works
E — Energy
Cognitive fuel, 0–100. Drains under load; mean-reverts toward C in ~2 h.
S — Stress
Nervous-system load, 0–100. Builds fast under pressure; decays over days, not hours.
P — Pressure
Weighted drain signal (0–1). High P burns E faster and keeps S elevated.
C — Circadian floor
Expected E at this hour based on your chronotype. E pulls toward it when pressure is low.

Energy-Stress Map

This is a phase portrait — a map of how your Energy (E) and Stress (S) evolve together in real time. Each arrow shows the direction you'd drift from that point if today's pace continued. Top-left (high E, low S) = restored and sharp. Bottom-right (low E, high S) = overloaded.

How the arrows are computed. Two differential equations run continuously: dE/dt (rate of energy change) and dS/dt (rate of stress change). Drain features — back-to-back meetings, heavy async load, late nights — push dE/dt negative and dS/dt positive. Recovery activities reverse both. Each arrow in the field is the vector (dE/dt, dS/dt) evaluated at that grid point.

The nullclines are where one variable pauses. The green vertical line is where dE/dt = 0 — left of it, energy falls; right of it, energy rises. The red horizontal line is where dS/dt = 0 — below it, stress rises; above it, stress falls. The lines shift as your activity pattern changes: more drain moves both lines toward the bottom-right corner.

Where they cross is your equilibrium — the (E, S) point today's pattern is slowly pulling you toward, even if you never reach it. The shaded square marks it. Your live dot shows where you are now.

Connecting to the model. Log energy or stress (E / S keys) to move the live dot. Add recovery activities (R key) to shift the nullclines up-left. Calibrate your chronotype (TUNE) to tune the circadian baseline C, which sets the energy nullcline position throughout the day.

What is Mental Meter?

A real-time dashboard that estimates your cognitive energy throughout the day — not from how you feel, but from what your computer has actually seen you do.

The core idea
Your brain has a fuel tank. Meetings, deep focus, context switching, and late hours drain it. Sleep, walks, food, and downtime refill it. Mental Meter tracks that tank in real time using a two-variable dynamical system (energy + stress) driven by ActivityWatch data.
Where the data comes from
ActivityWatch runs locally on your machine and watches which apps and windows are active. Mental Meter reads it every 5 minutes to estimate meeting load, focus duration, and context switching — then feeds those into the energy model.
The circadian layer
On top of your activity, your body has a natural 24-hour rhythm. The model shifts your resting energy baseline up or down depending on the time of day and your chronotype (lark, intermediate, or owl).
Three display layers
Tap the meter to go deeper. Layer 0 is just the fill. Layer 1 opens the diagnostics view: circadian curve, state tanks, energy dynamics, and phase portrait. Layer 2 adds the signal breakdown: pressure, drain rows, recovery rows, and the legacy activity window.
Research context
This tool is part of ongoing research at UC Berkeley on passive sensing and cognitive self-monitoring. The model is experimental — treat the number as a useful signal, not a medical measurement.

Glossary

E (Energy)
Your mental fuel tank on a 0-100 scale. When it drops, the meter shrinks; sleep, walks, food, and people refill it.
S (Stress)
A second tank that rises under cognitive load like stacked meetings, late-night work, or heavy multitasking. High stress makes energy drain faster.
Pressure
The load on you right now: meeting density, message volume, and app switching. When pressure stays high, stress climbs.
Chronotype
Your natural body-clock type: lark, intermediate, or owl. It sets when your daily energy baseline tends to peak.
MSF_sc
Sleep-corrected mid-sleep on free days, derived from the Munich Chronotype Questionnaire. We use it to place your chronotype curve.
Peak cognition
The part of the day when your circadian baseline is highest. Use it for demanding work when possible.
Recovery
Anything that adds energy back: outside time, exercise, social contact, food, or rest. Use recovery to push the meter upward.
Drain
Anything that pulls energy out: meetings, deep focus, context switching, or late hours. High drain makes the meter fall faster.
ActivityWatch
A local time tracker that watches your active windows on your machine. We read it to estimate how much you meet, focus, and switch.
Energy model
The math that updates energy and stress every tick. It turns recovery, drain, pressure, and resting baseline into meter movement.
Daily rhythm (circadian)
Your 24-hour resting energy baseline. It says what "rested" should feel like at this time of day — peaks mid-morning, dips in the afternoon.
Demo mode
A frontend-only simulation with no backend writes. Use it to try activities and watch the model respond in real time.

How it works

  1. Your energy level rises and falls through the day: drains pull it down, while rest, walks, food, and people refill the meter.
  2. A second tank tracks stress — back-to-back meetings and long focus sessions fill it, and a full stress tank makes energy drain faster.
  3. On top of all that, your body has a natural daily rhythm — your circadian baseline — that quietly lifts or lowers what "rested" means at this hour.
  4. Every five minutes the app reads what you actually did (meetings, focus blocks, walks, sleep) and updates both tanks a little.
  5. The meter fill shows your current energy level, smoothed; the detail layers show the math underneath if you want to look.

The math

Energy (E)
Tracks cognitive fuel — 100% is fully rested, 0% is depleted. It decays under D (drain) and recovers toward a circadian baseline (C) with a ~2-hour time constant. Two hours of low-pressure activity brings you most of the way back.
Stress (S)
Tracks load on your nervous system. It rises with meetings, notifications, and context-switching, then decays slowly — a natural half-life of around 3 days. Stress from a demanding week persists into the weekend even after energy has recovered, which is why feeling rested but still wired is physiologically normal.
D (drain)
A weighted sum of active drain inputs (meetings, async comms, deep work) minus a fraction of recovery inputs. Higher P means faster energy burn and faster stress accumulation.
Energy-Stress map
The phase portrait in layer 2 shows drift direction at every (E, S) point. The nullclines mark where each variable would stop changing; their intersection is the equilibrium — where your state would land if today's activity pattern continued indefinitely. A high-drain pattern pulls the equilibrium toward low-E, high-S. Recovery blocks shift it back. Watching where the equilibrium sits is often more useful than watching the instantaneous dot.

The phase diagram, plain English

The Energy-Stress map is a phase portrait — a picture of how two things (energy and stress) push and pull each other over time. You don't need to know any math to read it:

  • Each arrow shows which direction you'd move from that spot if you kept today's pace going.
  • The dashed lines (called nullclines) are the borders where one variable would stop changing. Energy stops changing on the horizontal dashed line; stress stops changing on the vertical one.
  • Where the dashes cross is your equilibrium — the resting point today's activity is slowly pulling you toward. A packed day pulls it down and right (low energy, high stress). Rest and recovery push it up and left.
  • The live dot is where you are right now. Watching where the equilibrium sits tells you more than the dot alone — it's the trajectory that matters, not just the snapshot.

Under the hood, the model runs two differential equations (rate equations) that update continuously. If you're curious about the exact formulas, tap the meter → More Info → signal breakdown.

How the Mental Meter model works

Your mental energy is modeled as two coupled tanks driven by what you actually do across the day. Six knobs explain almost everything you'll see.

Energy (E)

The main meter fill shows how much capacity you have left. Recovery activities lift it; drains pull it down. It also drifts back toward your resting baseline over time.

dE/dt = recovery − drain − k·(E − C)

Stress (S)

A second tank that builds under cognitive pressure (deep work, meetings, late-night focus). High S accelerates drain on E and shortens its recovery half-life.

dS/dt = D_in − decay·S

Pressure

The instantaneous load on you right now — meetings stacked, message volume, switching between tools. It's the input to S, not a tank itself. Think of it as "how loud the day is."

How activities change things

Each activity card flips a small set of feature inputs (right-side panel). Those flow through the same energy model math the live app uses. The right panel only shows the features the activity actually moves — others stay neutral.
